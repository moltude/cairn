What Cairn changes between CalTopo and onX

This page follows one real map, the Bitterroots sample from Cairn's tests, from a CalTopo export to onX-ready files. Every screenshot is the real app. The map has 177 markups in 10 folders: 68 waypoints, 92 lines and 17 areas.

  • 68waypoints that a plain CalTopo GPX or KML export would hand to onX with no icon
  • 43of those get a specific onX icon from Cairn right away, across 12 different icons
  • 25fall back to the default pin and are flagged for you to review
  • 10CalTopo folders, each its own numbered import

Computed by loading the fixture in the real app and reading its data (scripts/make_walkthrough_shots.py). "Would arrive with no icon" is the project's documented behavior of CalTopo's GPX and KML export, not something measured on this specific map in onX.

Before

What the plain export gives onX

onX Backcountry imports only GPX and KML. CalTopo's own export page says those two formats lose line and marker styles and folder organization. The one CalTopo export that keeps everything, GeoJSON, is the one onX can't open. Cairn reads all three, so you can start from any of them (GeoJSON keeps the most). A CalTopo GPX carries names, coordinates, notes and Garmin line-color names, and nothing onX can use for icons or folders.

Step 1

Export from CalTopo (GeoJSON is best) and add it to Cairn

One export, one file. Cairn runs entirely in your browser, so the map never leaves your computer.

What Cairn finds

Your folders and icons survive the trip

The 10 folders are on the left with their counts, and every waypoint shows its onX icon name next to a drawing of the marker. Cairn matched 43 of the 68 waypoints to a specific icon, such as Parking (10), Climbing (9), Summit (6) and Shelter (4), from the CalTopo symbol or words in the name. Hover a marker to see why it was chosen.

What Cairn flags

25 waypoints need a decision

When the CalTopo symbol is a plain point and the name gives no clue, Cairn doesn't guess. It marks the waypoint Default pin. Here that's 22 plain points ("El Capitan", "Talus field", "Bridge") and 3 flags that Cairn's mapping sends to the default pin on purpose. Tick Default pin in the Icon list to see only those.

What you fix

One at a time, or many at once

Click a marker to change that item's icon and color: onX's 95 icons and 10 waypoint colors.

For a batch, search, tick the rows and choose Icon & color…. The dialog shows each item now and after. Here the three Sheafman ridge flags become Summits in one step, taking the map from 25 flagged waypoints to 22.

Your folders

Sort items into folders

Each folder becomes one onX folder, so set them up here. Drag rows onto a folder in the Folders list; tick several first to move them together. Here the six Sheafman rows go onto + New folder.

Type the new folder's name and press Enter. Click a folder's name to rename it; a name that's taken offers to merge the two. More → Move to… does the same from the keyboard.

What you import

Ten imports, one per folder

onX makes one folder per import and names it by date, so Cairn gives you one set of files per folder: a box with its name, its files and a Download button. Import each folder's files together, tick the new-folder box, and rename the folder. instructions.txt in the zip says the same and lists every item. Lines go out as GPX tracks, areas as onX areas in GPX.

What this page can't show yet
